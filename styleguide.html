Pick lines

1,204 traced fibre lines picked. 482 of them are drawn in the model. The rest are left out of the drawing so it turns smoothly, but they pass through the loop too.

Drag a loop round lines on the model to pick them. Each loop adds to the pick. Dragging does not turn the brain while this is on.

The pick group (#g-pick, .pk-count) and the loop drawn on the model (#lasso, .lasso). "Pick lines" shows the accent edge while picking is on; the other buttons appear only when they can do something. The count line says how many traced fibre lines are picked and, for a thinned drawing, how many of them are drawn. The loop is a dashed accent line over a dark edge so it shows over bright lines. On phones each button is at least 40 px tall.

The slice group (.sl-row, .sl-show): one row a slice, its tick box, a slider that moves it (off while the slice is off) and which slice of how many, in mono. Then the menu of what the slices show, and the key: the legend with the three direction colours, made by the same rule that colours the slices, or one line saying what brightness means. Shown only for tracts made from a scan. On phones each control is at least 40 px tall.

Welcome to Tract Explorer

A brain tract is a bundle of nerve fibres that carries signals between parts of the brain.

This site is a learning tool for seeing them in 3D, not for diagnosis.

Words explained

Streamline · traced fibre line
A line traced along the fibres, worked out from a diffusion scan.
Voxel · 3D pixel
A tiny cube of the scan, often about 2 mm across.

On a computer the tract card (or the region card, on the Wiring tab) and the results box share one column on the right of the 3D view, card on top. The column starts under the view buttons and ends above the credit line, so it never covers either; the brain moves left to make room. When the two are taller than the space, the column scrolls and the card's buttons stay at the foot of the card (scroll this example). Both use the glass surface with a thin line and 10 px corners. On phones the card and the results box keep their own places.

DIFFUSION SCAN

Make tracts from a scan

You need a diffusion MRI scan. It measures how water moves in the brain. Water moves most easily along the fibres, so the scan shows which way they run.

  • The scan itself: a .nii or .nii.gz file
  • Its .bval file: the scan strength (b-value) of each image
  • Its .bvec file: the direction measured in each image

Files are read on this device and are not uploaded.

dwi.nii.gz
A grid of 96 × 96 × 60 tiny cubes (voxels), each 2.5 mm across.
65 images (volumes): 1 plain image (b = 0) and 64 at scan strength 1000 (b-value, in s/mm²).

A diffusion scan needs three files. Missing: the .bvec file. Choose all three files together.

Fold (.fold): more settings in named groups

Everything that is not needed to start sits in one fold, closed at first. Its label says how many settings it holds and how many were changed from where they started, and names them, so a change is not forgotten when the fold is closed. Inside, each group (.fold-g) has a heading and one line saying what it is for. When the panel has more below its footer, a fade marked "More below" sits over the footer's top edge (#maker.mk-below).
